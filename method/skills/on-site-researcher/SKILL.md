# On-site researcher

Digitized books end. Behind them stands a wall of paper that only a person in
a reading room can open: a local genealogist, an archive's own search
service, a parish, a school museum. Such a person reads the book with their
eyes and, more valuable than any photograph, knows what lies where for this
village and what never survived.

## When

- Coverage says the open sources of the place were **read**, not searched
  once: `research coverage --person <id>` shows passes with control, and the
  queue has no cheap cell left for this line.
- The missing paper is located (archive, fond, and if known inventory and
  book), or locating it is itself the question: what survives for this place
  and these years.
- Within the witness horizon the family was asked first. A relative's answer
  is free; a book is not.

Too early is the usual mistake: an agent an hour into a line is ready to write
to an archive about something a grandmother remembers or an open index holds.

## Where such people are

- **The regional forum thread of the district, county or parish.** The region
  pack's source cards name the forums. A post there is less a question to the
  crowd than a signal: people who work in that archive on those villages read
  the thread and answer privately.
- **The archive's own paid search.** Slower and official; the answer can be
  certified. Its order, price and queue belong in the pack's custody cards.
- **People of the place:** a local historian, the parish, a school or district
  museum. They hold lists, photographs and recordings no catalogue names.

## The opening post

Draft it for the host in the forum's language and manner:

- one family name with its recorded spellings, one place, the years;
- what is already read, so that nobody sells the open index back;
- the paper sought, as exactly as it is known;
- no living people and no whole tree.

The host posts under their own account. The agent does not register, sign in
or write as the host unless the host has set that up and said so.

## Working with the person

1. **Ask first what exists.** Which books of this parish or village council
   survive, for which years, in which fond; what was lost. Record the answer
   as an observation with its date and author, and offer it to the region
   pack: it serves every family of that place.
2. **Order by a unit both sides understand** — a book, a year, a page. Name
   the fond, inventory and file when known, the village, the surnames with
   variants, and the family's fingerprint of names so a namesake is not
   photographed instead.
3. **Say what to photograph:** the whole entry with the page header and the
   entries beside it, the cover or title of the file, the sheet number.
4. **Agree the price and the unit before the work.** The host pays. Keep the
   request in the family's requests with who, what, price, date and state.
5. **Write in their terms.** Metric book, confession list, revision tale,
   household book: the pack's vocabulary and the archive's inventory give the
   words. The agent drafts every reply; the host sends it.

## What comes back

- A photograph is a paper. Take it in with its original bytes; read it
  literally; give a load-bearing place a second reading. The helper's own
  transcription is a version beside yours, not the reading.
- "Nothing found" is a zero only with its coverage: which books, years and
  sheets were looked through. Without that it is an access gap.
- Advice ("that village was in the neighbouring parish until 1867") is a lead
  to verify, weighted like any other, and often the best result of the order.
- Images from an archive are for the family's own reading. Showing them
  outside the family follows the archive's terms, which the pack records.

## Budget and stop

- One unit at a time: the book that decides the leading version.
- Two orders that bring nothing: stop buying books and ask for advice.
- Prices change with demand. Record each price seen as a dated observation;
  never quote an old one to the host as current.
